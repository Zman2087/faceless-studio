STORY SERIES RULES (settings.format is "story": these override the channel defaults above where they differ)
- Each post is one PART of the serialized story in settings.story, planned by the Showrunner. Follow post.brief, post.scenes, post.choice and post.cameos exactly; never contradict settings.story.canon.
- THE MECHANICS ARE INVISIBLE. Viewers never see or hear meters, scores, stakes, flags, "suspicion", "proof", how many parts are left, or what endings exist. Nothing on screen, in narration, captions, replies or the cover hints that an option "raises" or "lowers" anything. Consequences are only ever shown as story (a siren closer, a face on a screen, a door that won't open). The only number viewers ever see is last part's real vote share.
- LENGTH: every part runs 75-150 seconds (post.length_s; never padded: dramatic pauses yes, filler no): about 150-380 spoken words, 20-50 shots, a new framing every 2-3 s. Structure: cold open on the consequence of last vote (0-8 s), escalation in 3-5 beats with at least one twist or reveal, the cliffhanger, then the choice (last 6-8 s, card held 3.5 s after the last word).
- CONSEQUENCES FIRST: the first 8-15 s of every part show and explain what the audience's last decision caused, as story ("You ran. Three streets later, every doorbell camera in Kellerman Lane had your face."), opening with the real vote share. Never as meters or "suspicion went up": only what happened, what it looks like, and what it means for "you" now.
- AUDIENCE NAMING: some characters are named by the audience (settings.story.cast[] with nameable: true). Always use a character's current name (cast[].name); story-map text uses their default_name, so swap it for the current name everywhere. When a part carries post.naming {target, prompt}, its captions ask for it ("Comment below: what's the victim's name?") and its last line can tease it; the next part's recap credits the audience ("You named him Marcus.") the moment that character first appears.
- NARRATE WHAT CAN'T BE SEEN: the picture shows what happens; the narrator only says what the viewer can't see: what "you" think and feel, what's at stake, what's about to go wrong, what nobody else knows, the clock. Never describe what's on screen (no "Headphones on. Hood up.", no "There's a man against the bins. Office shirt. Lanyard."), never mention the camera or the shot. Let characters speak their own lines (1-4 a part). Fewer, sharper narrator lines beat many: if a line only restates the image, cut it.
- THE HOOK (first 3 seconds decide everything): open IN THE MIDDLE OF IT, on the part's most charged image with "you" on screen from frame 0, and say the stakes in the first line, second person, in under 2 seconds ("You just stole ten million dollars." / "You're holding the knife."). Then 2-3 short concrete lines that make it real (where you are, what's in your hands, who's watching), then the turn ("Ninety seconds ago, you were just walking home." / "And the hard part? It starts now."). No scene-setting, no time-of-day openers, no slow build before the stakes. Later parts open with the consequence of the vote the same way.
- PACING: tight like the best interactive Shorts: the narrator talks most of the time (about two-thirds of the runtime), gaps between lines 0.2-0.4 s (only a planned dramatic beat runs longer, at most 2 per part), punchy sentences of 3-8 words. Music sits under the voice from frame 0 to the vote card (remotion/tools/score.py, about 12-14 dB under the voice, ducked under speech).
- VOICES: the narrator (settings.voice) is cinematic and engaging: varied pace, short punches, a beat of silence before a reveal, never flat. Full voice at all times: never whisper or go breathy (it sounds odd on a phone speaker); build tension with pace, pauses and emphasis instead. Write the script as beats, each with a speaker: "narrator" for most of it, and 1-4 short CHARACTER LINES per part (<= 10 words each) spoken by the character in their own voice (settings.story.cast[].voice), e.g. pike: "HEY! Come back here!" or hale: "You have something of mine, son." A character line shows as a speech bubble over the scene (tools/beats.mjs does this from the beat's speaker); put the speaker in the shot. Shouted lines are written in capitals with "!".
- Narration is second person ("you"), present tense, in settings.writing_voice. Line 1 (<= 8 words) is the hook; line 2 is post.recap: what the audience decided, with the real vote share ("71% of you said run. So you ran."), and, if post.feedback_nod is set, one short line that shows the audience was heard (a callback to a popular theory or comment, never a username unless that person bought a cameo). The last spoken line asks the choice, then stop: the vote card does the rest.
- Visuals depend on settings.story.look. ILLUSTRATED ("illustrated", settings.video.engine "remotion-illustrated"): a short cinematic of AI-generated graphic-novel stills in the series style (settings.story.style), animated in Remotion: each "media" shot gets zoom [from, to] toward a focus point (a slow push, or a punch-in on one part of the still: a face, a hand, the knife, a window), rain, flicker on practical lights, fx shake + flash on a shock, streaks on a runner, siren_rise (siren_level 0.6 → 1) over the last 10-15 s when police are coming, vignette + grade on every shot, and film grain. One still serves several shots by re-framing it (wide, then a punch-in, then another detail), so a part needs few stills: at most 6 NEW stills a part, everything else reused from earlier parts and the empty location plates (settings.story.plates). Every new still is generated with the hero sheet (cast you.sheet) and the key art (settings.story.key_art) attached as references so the hero and the places stay the same all season; frame to keep anything that contradicts the story (a police car that hasn't arrived yet) out of the crop. Speech bubbles for character lines; captions at y about 1470, never over a face. STICK ("stick", the default and cheapest; engine "remotion-scenes"): drawn and animated entirely in code (Remotion "scene" shots, remotion/SPEC.md): the cast on drawn sets, moody and cinematic, not childish: rain, fog, streetlight flicker, a rising red-blue siren wash (fx siren_rise), flash and shake on a shock, streaks on runners, spotlight, heartbeat, grain (spec grain: true), punch-ins and slow camera moves. CONSISTENT CAST: every character is defined once in the spec's cast from settings.story.cast[].stick (colour + outfit: hood, headphones, lanyard, tie, glasses, hair, pants, shoes) and never restyled per shot except for a real change (headphones on, then round the neck); the hero always has the grey hood up and the yellow headphones; use item and item2 when a character holds two things. Act every beat with poses (walk, run, kneel, kneel_reach, kneel_hold, pickup, frozen, look_up, hand_mouth, slumped…) and expressions; a lit "window" prop for a witness. Violence implied only (slumped, never wounded). Either way real people, gore, weapons in use and on-screen injuries never appear: violence is implied by cuts, sound and a reaction. ALL TEXT IS REMOTION: no words, letters or logos inside generated art.
- SHORT-FORM STYLE (every part): 9:16; the hook frame is frame one (no fade from black) and the first line lands in the first 1-2 s; a new framing every 2-3 s (one shot per beat, punch-ins count); captions in the safe zone below the action, never over faces; the poster title card (kicker = season title, PART <n>, the part title) on screen for about 2 s right after the first two lines, never over the hook; character lines as speech bubbles; any naming prompt as a headline overlay while that character is first on screen; the A/B/C vote card big and readable, held 4-5 s after the last word; tighten pauses longer than about 0.7 s except deliberate dramatic beats (a silence before a scream); the last framing matches the first so the video loops; never any "AI-generated" text on screen.
- WRITER OUTPUT for a part: beats.json for remotion/tools/beats.mjs (see the video-script skill): theme = settings.brand, cast = settings.story.cast mapped to {color, outfit}, cast_names, titlecard {text, sub "Part <n>"}, choice {text, options}, and beats [{say, speaker, shot (a "scene" shot with "relative": true so its timings are fractions of the beat, see remotion/SPEC.md), bubble {x, y} for character lines, cameo {text, handles} on the beat where the cameo appears, sound [{src: settings.story.sfx[<name>], offset}] for sirens, footsteps, doors, a heartbeat}]. Draft timing at 2.5 words/s; the producer re-times to the real voice. ILLUSTRATED parts instead give each beat a media shot {type: "media", still: "<key>", move, focus {x, y} (0-1, where the eye should go), rain?, flicker?} and list the stills in post.stills [{key, prompt (the scene in one or two sentences: who is where doing what, framing, light; never the style words, the producer adds them), cast [ids in the shot], location (a settings.character.location_stills key), reuse (the key or job id of an existing still to reuse instead of generating, when the place and framing already exist)}]: 8-12 new stills per part at most, reusing location stills and earlier parts' stills wherever the scene allows; a still can carry 2-3 beats with different moves and focus points.
- Production (producer): no Higgsfield video generation for story parts. Voice: split the beats into takes by speaker, in order (consecutive narrator beats form one take); generate each take with that speaker's voice (narrator = settings.voice with delivery settings.voice.delivery; characters = settings.story.cast[].voice); CHECK EVERY TAKE WORD FOR WORD: transcribe each take with faster-whisper and compare it to the script line (ignoring punctuation, numbers as words); a take with a missing, added or changed word (e.g. "should" for "shouldn't") is regenerated once; join them with `node remotion/tools/voice-track.mjs takes.json voice.mp3` (per-take "gap" for dramatic pauses and room for title cards and shocks); transcribe voice.mp3 with faster-whisper to words.json (drop the character lines' words from the captions: their bubbles carry them); then `node remotion/tools/beats.mjs beats.json spec.json --words words.json`. Render with `node remotion/tools/render-long.mjs spec.json final.mp4` (chunks of ~25 s, stitched, full audio mixed back); run it as a background sandbox job (size "xl" for parts over 120 s) and poll. Everything except the voices costs zero credits. Generate a Higgsfield still only if a part's brief explicitly needs a one-off image the scene engine can't draw, and say why in production_notes. ILLUSTRATED parts: generate post.stills that have no reuse in ONE Higgsfield generate_image_batch with settings.video (model, quality, resolution, aspect 9:16), prompt = still.prompt + " " + settings.story.style.prompt + " No text, no letters, no logos.", and medias = the image_references of EVERY cast member in the still (cast[].reference job ids; the hero's sheet always when "you" is in it) plus settings.story.key_art as a style reference. Announce the credit cost first (est_credits_per_still x count) and stay under budget.max_credits_per_post. Look at every result (sandbox_exec with image_paths): regenerate a still once if a character's signature look drifted (the hero's grey hood and mustard-yellow headphones), the art contains text, or anything graphic slipped in. Download the stills into the sandbox (curl the result URL; cloud workspaces that can't reach the image host get the file back by resizing to 1080×1920 JPEG at quality ~78 and returning it with sandbox_exec image_paths, at most 512 KB per call), set each shot's src to its file, and save the job ids on the post (post.stills[].job_id) so later parts can reuse them. At most 6 new stills a part; re-frame stills with zoom/focus punch-ins instead of generating more. SOUND BED (free, made with ffmpeg in the sandbox, never paid audio): rain or city ambience (filtered pink noise plus a low brown-noise rumble), a low tense drone (detuned sine tones under a lowpass) that cuts out at the part's shock moment and returns after, and timed sounds: a short filtered hit on the shock, whooshes on a few cuts, footsteps (short low thumps at a run cadence), a light-switch click, rising two-tone sirens (an FM sine wail, lowpassed, fading up). Loudness-normalise the beds to -16 LUFS, then mix them low: music_volume about 0.05 (-26 dB) and ambience_volume about 0.04 (-28 dB) with duck 0.6, so the voice always leads; finish the master at about -14 LUFS integrated (ffmpeg loudnorm on the final audio). Check the mix by ear in the preview.
- ELEVENLABS VOICES (when a voice's provider is "elevenlabs"): creative_generate_speech with that voice_id, model from the voice (default eleven_v4), generations_count 1, and NO style tags for the narrator by default (read the text plainly in his natural voice; add a tag only if settings.voice.tags allows it); character lines may use [breathless], [shouting], [panicked]; never [whispering]; one call per take, never re-call to retry (it charges again); poll creative_get_flow_run_status, download the audio, then continue with voice-track.mjs. ElevenLabs is the default for story series because it doesn't spend Higgsfield credits.
- Remotion spec for every part: a titlecard overlay at 0 (ILLUSTRATED: style "poster", kicker = the season title (settings.story.season.title), number = the part number, text = the part's title, end about 5 s; STICK: settings.story.title split into two lines with a pipe, sub "Part <n>"), each cameo as a cameo overlay during the scene it belongs to (text = the in-world label of the placement, e.g. "Neighbours who heard it"), and a choice overlay for the last 4 s (ILLUSTRATED: style "poster"; text = choice.text, all of the choice options, sub "Comment A or B" or "Comment A, B or C"). ILLUSTRATED specs also set grain: true, music/ambience with settings.story.audio volumes and duck, and captions y about 1340. Captions on. No meter overlay, ever. THE COVER: render the part's cover with Remotion from its strongest still (the hero visible, the top third calm): `node remotion/render.mjs cover.json --still cover.jpg` where cover.json is a 1-second spec with that still (move "still") and the same poster titlecard (y 150, size 0.86), plus a 1280x720 YouTube version (width 1280, height 720, focus on the hero, titlecard align "right", size 0.62, y 70). Look at both before saving them to the post (cover_url, cover_youtube_url).
- Story content rules: fiction only, not based on or resembling a real case or real people; violence implied, never shown (no blood, wounds or bodies on screen); no procedural detail that would teach anyone to commit or hide a crime (choices stay at the level of a decision, never a method); police and real institutions are not shown as corrupt in a way that targets real forces; cameo handles are never suspects, killers, victims or accused.
- QA captions for a part: first line is the hook; then "Part <n>. Last time <recap>."; then the choice spelled out and how to vote: "Comment A, B or C (whichever letters this part has). Votes close at <settings.story.vote.closes_text>." plus, on Instagram, "or vote in our story poll"; suggest a pinned comment with the same choice; for parts with cameos add "Featuring" + the handles (they will share it). Also write story_poll: {question, options} for an Instagram story / YouTube community poll with the same choice. Never mention meters, endings or how many parts are left.
- ROUTE REVEAL posts (kind "reveal"): the season's bonus episode, made only after the season has ended. It may finally show the perfect route and the vote that cost the audience (that is the point of it) but still never shows meters or numbers other than real vote shares. Narration follows post.brief and uses only the numbers in post.route. Second person, warm but teasing ("You were one vote from Cleared. Part 5. 67% of you took the cash."). The Remotion spec is post.remotion_spec as given: add the narration as audio and its words; change nothing else.

- THE SEASON BOOK: once a season has ENDED (settings.story.season.status "ended") and its pick-your-path book is listed (settings.story.book.url or settings.site.shop), the Route Reveal, the season recap and every season-end post carry ONE line about it, in the narrator's voice: "Every path you didn't take is in the book. Link in bio." (and the same line in the captions). Never mention the book, its endings or other paths while a season is live.
